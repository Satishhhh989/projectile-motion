Real-World Applications

  • Sports: Calculating the arc of a basketball shot, the trajectory of a golf ball, or the path of a kicked football.
  • Military: Ballistics for firing artillery shells and missiles to accurately hit targets.
  • Fountains: Designing water fountains where jets of water follow parabolic paths to create beautiful displays.
  • Aerospace: While orbital mechanics are more complex, initial rocket launch phases follow projectile principles before escaping Earth's gravity.

Formula Derivations

Projectile motion is analyzed by separating it into horizontal (x) and vertical (y) components. Horizontal motion has constant velocity, while vertical motion has constant downward acceleration (g).

1. Time of Flight (T): This is the total time the object is in the air. We find this by analyzing the vertical motion. The displacement in y is zero when it lands back at its starting height. $$y(t) = (v_0 \sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$$ Setting $y(t) = 0$: $$0 = t(v_0 \sin\theta - \frac{1}{2}gt)$$ This gives two solutions, $t=0$ (launch) and the time of flight: $$T = \frac{2v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$$

2. Maximum Height (H): This occurs at the peak of the trajectory, where the vertical velocity $v_y$ is momentarily zero. $$v_y(t) = v_0 \sin\theta - gt$$ Set $v_y(t) = 0$ to find the time to reach the peak, $t_{peak} = \frac{v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$, which is exactly $T/2$. Substitute this time back into the vertical position equation: $$H = y(t_{peak}) = (v_0 \sin\theta)\left(\frac{v_0 \sin\theta}{g}\right) - \frac{1}{2}g\left(\frac{v_0 \sin\theta}{g}\right)^2$$ $$H = \frac{(v_0 \sin\theta)^2}{g} - \frac{(v_0 \sin\theta)^2}{2g} = \frac{(v_0 \sin\theta)^2}{2g}$$

3. Horizontal Range (R): This is the total horizontal distance traveled. It's the horizontal velocity (which is constant) multiplied by the total time of flight, T. $$R = x(T) = (v_0 \cos\theta) \times T$$ Substitute the formula for T: $$R = (v_0 \cos\theta) \left(\frac{2v_0 \sin\theta}{g}\right) = \frac{v_0^2 (2\sin\theta\cos\theta)}{g}$$ Using the trigonometric identity $\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$: $$R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$$
